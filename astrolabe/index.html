Download each part as SVG, or print them all at true size (set the printer to 100% / actual size, not "fit to page"). Print the front (mater and plate) and the back on card and glue them back to back. Print the rete and the rule on transparency, and the alidade on card. Cut them out and fix them through the centres with a pin or a small bolt, in this order: alidade, back/front, rete, rule.

How it is drawn

The sky is projected stereographically from the pole you cannot see onto the plane of the equator, as on historical astrolabes: circles stay circles and angles are kept. The plate's edge is the tropic farthest from your pole. On it are the horizon, almucantars (circles of equal altitude), azimuths numbered from north through east, the twilight lines (Sun 6°, 12° and 18° down), and below the horizon the unequal (planetary) hours, traced exactly, not with the classical three-point circles. The limb is marked in hours (noon at the top) and degrees.

The rete carries the ecliptic, graduated in longitude and in the Sun's dates for the chosen year, a right-ascension rim, and the traditional fixed stars (the stars of medieval retes and of astrology; the royal and Behenian stars in bold) from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, carried to the chosen year by proper motion and precession. Southern latitudes get the mirror-image southern astrolabe. The back has an altitude scale, the zodiac and a calendar for the chosen year (the alidade across a date shows the Sun's degree), and a shadow square.

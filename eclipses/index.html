How these are computed

Eclipses are found with astronomy-engine's searches. The Saros series is the eclipse's lunation count from a reference eclipse (eclipses one saros, 223 lunations, apart share a series; one inex, 358 lunations, apart are in consecutive series), numbered as in NASA's canons. Gamma, magnitudes, the hybrid test, the contacts and the path are worked from the shadow's geometry with the same Sun, Moon and Earth figures astronomy-engine uses; the central duration is the local eclipse at the point of greatest eclipse. The path's limits are drawn where the edge of the umbra (or antumbra) meets the ground, the partial zone from a 2° grid, so both are approximate near the path's ends and the zone's edge. A penumbral lunar eclipse is called an appulse, as in Solar Fire. The Position is the eclipse degree at the exact lunation: the conjunction for a solar eclipse, the Moon's place for a lunar one.

The ecliptic limits on the wheel are the classical ones, from the extremes of the Moon's and Sun's parallax and semidiameter and of the Moon's inclination: within about 15°21' of a node a New Moon always eclipses the Sun somewhere, beyond about 18°31' never; for the Moon's umbra 9°30'-12°15', her penumbra about 15½°-18½°.

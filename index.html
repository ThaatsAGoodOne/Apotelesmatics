    Primary Directions -- General Condition

    About this technique

    Zodiacal directions use the classical "arc of direction" technique, read directly from al-Qabisi, Introductions to Astrology IV.11-14 (Dykes trans.) -- one of several astrologers who transmit this method, not its inventor. A significator is directed forward to a target degree, and the arc between them converts to time at one year per degree (al-Qabisi's own explicit sub-degree rate: a month per 5', 6 days per 1', i.e. a flat 360-day nominal year). The arc itself is a proportional blend of a pure right-ascension arc and a pure oblique-ascension arc, weighted by how far the significator sits through its own quadrant (its semi-diurnal or semi-nocturnal arc) -- reducing exactly to a plain right-ascension arc when the significator sits on the Midheaven or IC, and to a plain oblique-ascension arc on the Ascendant or Descendant. Five traditional significators can be directed (Ascendant: body; Sun: honor and dignity; Moon: soul and marriage; Lot of Fortune: wealth; Midheaven: career and mastery), plus two further points offered here using the same technique, beyond al-Qabisi's own five: the Lot of Spirit and the prenatal Syzygy. Each is directed through either of two targets: the bound (term) lord of wherever the significator currently sits, walking sign by sign through successive bounds ("distribution through the bounds," with a secondary "lord of the rays" annotation whenever some other planet's own aspect ray to the significator happens to perfect during that same bound period); or successive planetary aspect rays (conjunction/sextile/square/trine/opposition), nearest first. Whichever bounds system is selected above is used. Every point involved -- bound cusps, aspect rays, the significator itself -- is treated as if it sat exactly on the ecliptic; exact for the Ascendant/Midheaven/bound-cusps, but an approximation whenever a real planet is directed to another real planet, since planets have their own ecliptic latitude. This is the natal "general condition" only -- profection-scaled and solar-return-scaled variants are a separate, later feature. When the significator falls in the descending quadrant (houses 7-9, from the Descendant to the Midheaven), Ibn Ezra's account of Ptolemy directs it backward (against the order of the signs) instead of forward -- a genuinely disputed point in the tradition itself (Dorotheus argued for always-forward directing), offered here as an option rather than a correction.

    Mundane directions use Placidus Semi-Arc, a genuinely latitude-aware technique using each point's true right ascension and declination instead of the ecliptic-only approximation above: the promissor's own proportional position through its semi-arc (diurnal if above the horizon, nocturnal if below) is projected onto the significator's own semi-arc, and the arc of direction is the difference between that projected position and the significator's own natal one. Read here directly from Martin Gansten's Primary Directions (Appendix I, "proportional semi-arcs"), a modern source documenting Placidus's own method -- the term applies regardless of latitude data, so it's labeled the same either way. Real planetary latitude is always used here (only the Moon, among the seven significators, actually has any -- the Ascendant, Midheaven, Sun, Lot of Fortune, Lot of Spirit, and prenatal Syzygy all sit exactly on the ecliptic by definition): that's the entire point of a mundane technique, so there's no separate latitude-on/off toggle -- pick Zodiacal above instead for an ecliptic-only technique. There is no mundane walk through the bounds: the source computes single planet-to-planet arcs directly (its own worked example is one such arc), and an earlier attempt here to extend it into a bound-by-bound walk produced non-increasing ages partway through the zodiac -- a real bug, not a rare edge case, so that extension has been dropped rather than left half-working. Instead, Bound Distribution under Mundane walks the bounds zodiacally (as under Zodiacal) and fills each bound's Lord of Rays with the mundane directions (by whichever technique is selected) that perfect within it -- so the bound lords and the mundane aspects can be read together without switching types. Every walk is computed either fully direct (promissor carried to the significator) or fully converse (significator carried to the promissor) -- Martin Gansten's own distinction, Chapter 1 of Primary Directions -- decided once for the whole significator, not per event, shown in the table's own Motion column. Defaults by hemisphere (eastern significators direct, western converse) and is switchable below. The Ascendant and Midheaven use a different, simpler formula from the rest (Gansten's own sec. 2/3, "directing a point to the meridian/horizon") rather than the proportional semi-arc projection above, since that projection is explicitly scoped by its own source to non-angular points only. For Ray Crossing specifically, a mundane sextile/square/trine/opposition is not an ecliptic-longitude offset the way it is under Zodiacal above -- for the Ascendant and Midheaven it's a proportion of each classical planet's own semi-arc in right-ascension space (Raphael's classical semi-arc-trisection house-cusp construction,Star Manual, "Of Mundane Primary Directions to the Angles"), reproducing a real chart's own published mundane directions exactly once implemented this way (an earlier version here used the ecliptic-offset construction and disagreed with real published output by tens of degrees for several events). Ray Crossing for the Sun/Moon/Lot of Fortune significators still uses the older ecliptic-offset construction for now -- extending the semi-arc trisection to planet-to-planet aspects (not just planet-to-angle) needs to handle a promissor's ray crossing into an adjacent quadrant with a different semi-arc, which the angular case never faces, and there's no published reference data on hand yet to verify it against.

    Mundane's own Technique choice affects a non-angular significator's own CONJUNCTION/OPPOSITION candidate: Placidus Semi-Arc (the default) is the construction described just above, with Gansten's own Placidean "under the pole" (4b) substituted in for the one specific case that construction is known to get wrong.Regiomontanus instead always uses Gansten's Regiomontanian circles-of-position formula (4c, the same construction the Zodiacal Technique select's own Regiomontanus option uses, fed real mundane right ascension/declination) for conjunction/opposition, regardless of that known-bad case -- with an extra correction of its own, an antipodal substitution for a promissor that natally sits closer to the significator's opposite degree than to the significator itself (this formula, unlike plain 4a, has no automatic conjunction/opposition duality built in). Regiomontanus also replaces sextile/square/trine with a genuine circles-of-position construction (finding the significator's own equatorial division point via the horizon's north/south-point axis, then locating each promissor's own crossing of the division points 60/90/120 degrees around it) rather than the semi-arc-trisection construction Placidus Semi-Arc keeps using for that case. All five aspects are verified against a real 11-row Janus table (Sun significator, converse motion, a reference nativity) to within a few arcseconds.

    The Ascendant and Descendant (angular significators) get the same all-five-aspects treatment under Regiomontanus, via the same underlying construction (an angle's own equatorial division point is simply fixed by definition -- 90 degrees from RAMC for the Ascendant -- rather than needing to be located from its real declination the way a moving planet's does) -- conjunction/square/opposition happen to reduce to the exact same values Placidus Semi-Arc already gives (every classical quadrant house system necessarily agrees on the four angles themselves, diverging only in how each trisects a quadrant's interior), while sextile/trine genuinely differ. Verified against a real 15-row Janus table (Ascendant significator, direct motion) to within a few arcseconds on every row. The Midheaven and IC keep using Placidus Semi-Arc's own construction regardless of this choice -- the same derivation would be expected to extend there too, but no real data confirms it yet.

    Both types share one further choice: the Key, which converts a computed arc (always in degrees, under the technique's own universal "1 degree of arc = 1 year of life" premise) into an actual calendar date -- historically disputed, since astrologers have disagreed about how many real days make up that "year." Ptolemy uses a flat 360-day nominal year (al-Qabisi's own explicit rate, this app's original default). Naibod uses the Sun's own mean tropical motion instead (~370.6 real days per degree) -- the most common key in modern practice. Kepler uses the Sun's true (not mean) motion on the day of birth specifically, which varies by nativity. Placidus is a two-step day-for-a-year translation, not a single scaled rate (Martin Gansten's own account, Primary Directions, "The key of Placidus"): first find the number of real days after birth for the Sun's own true right ascension to advance by an amount equal to the arc's own degree value, then treat that day-count directly as a year-count and convert it to real elapsed time the ordinary way. An earlier version here wrongly modeled Placidus as "Naibod, but with the Sun's true motion instead of mean motion" -- a single equation that happened to look plausible but disagreed with a real chart's own published Placidus-keyed directions by months to over a year; the current implementation reproduces all 15 of that chart's dates to within a day. Customlets you enter your own rate directly, in degrees of arc per tropical year (1 for Ptolemy-equivalent, ~0.9856 for Naibod-equivalent).

      Distributions (Umar al-Tabari)

      About this technique

      Directs the solar return chart's own significator "by equal degrees" (plain ecliptic distance, no ascensional projection) through successive planetary rays or bound cusps, an entire day for every 59' 8" of arc, until completing the full 360° back to the significator's own starting degree (about one real year) -- Umar al-Tabari,Condition of the Native II.5 and Annual Methods II.2-3 (which describe the Ascendant specifically; extended here to the same significators the natal Directions panel offers). Converse Motion is an optional extension neither source describes: it carries the significator backward through the zodiac instead of forward, using the same arc formula as the natal Directions panel's own zodiacal converse. Converse in Western Half (on by default) picks it automatically, like the Directions panel: converse for a significator in the solar return chart's western half, direct for one in the eastern half.

        Cardinal Ingress

        The (real) Sun's entry into 0° Aries (the "Year Chart," vernal equinox), Cancer (summer solstice), Libra (autumnal equinox), or Capricorn (winter solstice) -- the classical quarterly mundane ingress charts.

        Jupiter-Saturn Mean Conjunction

        The conjunction of the mean (idealized, constant-rate) longitudes of Jupiter and Saturn -- Benjamin Dykes, Astrology of the World II: Revolutions & History, Introduction §5 and Appendix A. The traditional "Great Conjunction" series conjunctionist mundane astrology tracks by triplicity, distinct from Jupiter and Saturn's real (true) conjunction, which can occur up to 3 times in a row around one mean conjunction.

        Hyleg & Alcocoden

        About these methods

        'Umar al-Tabari (Omar Tiberiades), Three Books on Nativities I.4, "On the hilaj and kadukhudhah" (Dykes trans., Persian Nativities II). Dorotheus, Carmen Astrologicum III.1-2, "The releaser and house-master" (Dykes trans.) -- his releaser rule differs from Umar's (Ascendant/Midheaven/11th only, both luminaries always tested; the Ascendant is a last resort, not interleaved with Fortune); his own "years of life" technique is an ascensional distribution through the bounds, not a lookup table, and isn't implemented yet -- the Dorotheus option below still uses the shared table for that part. William Lilly, Christian Astrology III, ch. CIV-CV, "Of the Prorogator of Life" -- his hylegiacal places are wider (1st, 7th, 9th, 10th, or 11th, or the 12th within 25 degrees of the Ascendant), the luminary test is house-placement only (no dignity-lord-aspect requirement), and when neither luminary qualifies he searches the 7 classical planets for whichever holds the most combined essential dignity across three reference degrees rather than trying a fixed list of candidate degrees; his alcochodon never fails to resolve, falling back to whichever dignity lord scores highest even if none aspect the hyleg. He too cites his own years-table (not transcribed here), so the Lilly option below also uses the shared table. al-Qabisi (Alcabitius),Introduction to Astrology IV.4-IV.6 (Dykes trans., in Introductions to Astrology: Abu Ma'shar & al-Qabisi) -- the Sun and Moon get different house tests depending on whether each is the primary sect light or the backup; the dignity-lord gate tests 5 lords (adding the face lord to the usual domicile/exaltation/triplicity/bound); the final Ascendant fallback is established completely unconditionally, with no dignity gate at all; and a releasing luminary in its own domicile or exaltation sign (Sun in Leo/Aries, Moon in Cancer/Taurus) is explicitly both releaser and alcocoden together. No numeric years-table appears in the provided pages either, so the Alcabitius option also uses the shared table. Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos III.10, "Of Length of Life" (Robbins trans.) -- the historical ancestor of every method above. His 5 prorogative places (1st, 7th, 9th, 10th, 11th) are ranked by "power of domination" (Midheaven > Ascendant-zone > 11th > Descendant > 9th), and when more than one candidate independently qualifies, the one in the more authoritative place wins -- unlike every other method here, which just takes the first eligible candidate in a fixed order. His dignity-search fallback (when neither luminary qualifies) counts plain dignity-lordships rather than a weighted score, needs at least 3 of 5 to qualify at all, and -- for a day nativity specifically -- uses the Ascendant rather than the Part of Fortune as its third reference degree. Ptolemy has no alcocoden concept of his own: his years figure comes from a primary-directions arc (not yet implemented, same gap as every method above), so this option reuses 'Umar al-Tabari's own alcocoden procedure and the shared years table as a pragmatic stand-in. Vettius Valens, AnthologiesIII.1-3, 5 (his own PRIMARY technique only -- Book III also contains several later alternate methods, e.g. from Critodemus and Petosiris, deliberately left out of scope for now). Unlike every method above, this one has no equivalent of a fixed hylegiacal-house list: a preliminary "control" step (distilled to its stated principle, not the ~20-case literal list) decides which luminary -- or the Ascendant -- is dominant, then the term-lord of that point's degree becomes the "houseruler" -- unless it's below the horizon, in the Descendant, or in mutual reception ("exchange of terms") with the domicile lord of the controlling point's sign, in which case the nativity is judged to "lack a houseruler" outright (cross- checked directly against three of Valens' own worked examples). Years come from a genuine ascensional-arc computation (not a table lookup at all) from the apheta to the point square with it, or to the IC for an Ascendant apheta, compared against a second calculation from the houseruler: a fixed 76-year reference span (taken directly from Valens' own worked example) if it's in "operative degrees" (a trisection of each angle-to-angle arc, per his own stated preference among two methods he gives), otherwise a proportional deduction by its angular distance from the nearest angle -- whichever of the two component figures is less. An unprevented malefic (or the non-apheta luminary) aspecting the apheta within his own explicit 3-degree-each-side anaeretic zone can curtail the arc further, simplified from Valens' own fuller sign-by-sign scan to checking just the apheta's own degree.

        Anaeretic directions. The hyleg is directed by primary directions to the rays of the anaretas (killing planets): Saturn and Mars always; Mercury, the Sun and the Moon only when made malefic -- when the closest of their configurations with Jupiter, Venus, Saturn or Mars (whole-sign aspect within the two planets' combined moieties) is with Saturn or Mars -- plus the Sun's conjunction against a Moon hyleg, which combusts it, whatever the Sun's condition. No planet counts against itself as hyleg. Every ray counts; conjunction, square and opposition are the strong ones, sextile and trine the weak ones, and a direction that brings the hyleg into the bound of Saturn or Mars is marked as aggravated. The alcocoden's years are treated as a protected span: directions falling within them are listed but not taken to end life. After they have run, each direction to an anareta is a possible length of life, the first of them the earliest. The directions follow the Primary Directions panel's settings (type, technique, key, Max Age, and the descending-quadrant rule), always as Ray Crossing; direct or converse follows that panel's hemisphere rule applied to the hyleg itself (eastern direct, western converse) unless its Converse Motion box has been set by hand.

        Almuten & Victor of the Geniture

        About this contest

        Two distinct classical contests for "Lord/Almuten of the Geniture," selectable below. Ibn Ezra'sBook of Nativities Almuten Figuris: positive-only essential dignity (rulership/exaltation/ triplicity/term/face, 5/4/3/2/1), summed across the 5 traditional hylegiacal points (Ascendant, Sun, Moon, Lot of Fortune, prenatal Syzygy).         Financial Significator (Wealth)

        About this method

        Guido Bonatti, Book of Astronomy, Treatise 9, "On the Second House," Ch. 1 (pp.1198-1203): finding the significator of wealth is a cascade, not a single dignity sum. Take one candidate point at a time -- first the Lot of Fortune ("preferred to the Part of Substance... because the Part of Fortune signifies the native's substance, prosperity, and status, and his whole condition," p.1200) -- and test its own dignity rulers in order (sign, exaltation, night/day/participating triplicity, term, face): the first one that is not impeded and also aspects the point itself is the significator. Only if every one of a point's rulers fails does the cascade fall through to the next point -- here, the Lot of Substance (Bonatti's text continues further, to the 2nd sign/its Lord/an occupant, then Jupiter, then the sect light, not implemented here). "Impeded" follows the classical debilities: a hard aspect (conjunction/square/opposition) to Mars or Saturn, conjunction/opposition to the Moon's North Node, cadency (houses 3/6/9/12), retrogradation, or combustion -- a currently separating hard aspect afflicts at once, while an applying one only counts if it actually perfects and isn't itself frustrated by some other, sooner-completing aspect. The exact ordering of the 7 dignity-ruler tests and this specific "impeded" checklist aren't spelled out at this level of detail in Bonatti's own prose (which just says "impeded" and trusts the reader to know the standard classical debilities) -- both are cross-checked against a worked trace (Janus astrology software) that implements this same cascade, confirmed to use the older 3-ruler Dorothean triplicity table Bonatti himself transmits (not Ptolemy/Lilly's simplified 2-ruler one).

        Method. "Bonatti" (the default) follows his own text: first the al-mubtazz over the point -- "consider which of the aforesaid significators is stronger in that place by the multitude of dignities or strengths, because he will be called the al-mubtazz and will be the significator" (p.1200), the aforesaid being the lords of its domicile, exaltation, bound and triplicity, counted by how many of those dignities each holds -- then, if it is impeded or doesn't aspect, the lords in his own order: domicile, exaltation, bound, triplicity, and "lastly the Lord of the face" (p.1201), with the single triplicity lord of the chart's sect. A planet already tested isn't tested again. The Lot of Substance is treated the same way. If both Lots fail, the Bonatti method continues through his later steps (pp.1202-1205), stopping at the first that names a significator: the 2nd sign (a benefic in it -- Jupiter, the sect light, Venus -- or its Lord); Jupiter; the sect light in a good place; the al-mubtazz over the place of either Lot's al-mubtazz; the Lord of the Moon's domicile in a good place; any planet in the 2nd; the Lord of the Ascendant if it also rules the 4th; the Lords of the triplicity of the 2nd, of the sect light's sign, and of the Ascendant (all three Lords each, since Bonatti judges how many are sound -- "in two parts of his life, but not in the third"); and the Lords of the signs of Fortune and of Substance in a good place. Each must be free from impediments (the same checklist as above). "Fixed Order" instead tests the rulers in the worked trace's own order -- sign, exaltation, the three triplicity rulers (night, day, participating), term, face -- which reproduces that trace, and stops at the Lot of Substance. Bonatti writes "the second", "the 4th domicile" (domus) and "the house of substance" -- never a sign or a cusp outright; "[sign]" is Dykes' own insertion, and his notes argue for whole signs (with quadrant houses and no intercepted signs, the Lord of the Ascendant could never also rule the 4th). So places are by whole sign unless the quadrant-houses box is ticked, which uses the chart's own houses (with the Cusp Offset) and the Lords of the signs on their cusps.

        Profession Significator

        About this method

        Guido Bonatti, Book of Astronomy, Treatise 9, "On the Tenth House," Ch. 1 (pp.1337-1342+): Bonatti's own prose describes an elaborate multi-criteria procedure for finding the significator of the native's profession/work, but a worked trace (Janus astrology software) implementing this same chapter shows a much simpler actual procedure: pool whichever classical planets occupy the 1st, 10th, 7th, and 4th houses (using this page's own Cusp Offset control, like the other "N-degree rule" features here -- the worked trace itself used a fixed 10°), then -- if more than one candidate -- take whichever has the highest combined essential + accidental dignity (the same philosophy as the Wealth Significator above). This doesn't fully reproduce that trace's own specific numbers or, under every bounds system, its own stated winner -- see the code's own notes -- since Janus evidently uses its own separate internal scoring; this page uses one consistent dignity formula throughout instead of chasing that. If none of the 4 houses has an occupant, this falls back to whichever planet sits closest to the sect light (Sun by day, Moon by night) or the Lord of the Lot of Fortune, whichever is better disposed -- an untested branch, since the reference case always finds an occupant.

        Scoring. "Bonatti: dignities where it stands" (the default) follows his own words (p.1339): "Which if there were more of the planets in the aforesaid places, you will prefer, to all others, him who had dignities of greater authority there, or more [of them]" -- only essential dignities at the planet's own place count, ranked first by the highest-authority dignity held (domicile, then exaltation, triplicity, bound, face), then by how many it holds; accidental dignity and debilities play no part. "Essential + accidental" is the formula described above, the one that matches the worked trace. The same scoring decides the fallback and the Class of Profession below.

        Speculum

        About this table

        A "speculum" -- the per-body data table classical/Placidean primary-directions practice is built on, modeled on a real Janus software printout for a real reference nativity. Most columns follow standard spherical astronomy (right ascension, declination, meridian distance/semi-arc, oblique ascension/ descension, real topocentric azimuth/altitude); several (ADA, Pole, LAD, PMP, PHA, and this planet's own apparent Node/Perihelion/Aphelion) have no standard published formula and were reverse engineered directly against that printout, column by column -- see speculum.ts's own doc comments for exactly how confidently each one was verified. Zenith/Nadir Distance (ZD/ND) is a documented placeholder (plain topocentric 90°-altitude): the reference's own value is evidently a different, mundane construction that couldn't be reverse engineered in the time available. Dist% is likewise approximate, using each body's own fixed reference distance range rather than a per-chart search. This app only computes the Moon's MEAN node elsewhere (not the True node the reference itself uses), so the Moon's own node-adjacent numbers here differ from a True-node tool by up to about 1.5°.         Hayz

        About this determination

        William Lilly, Christian Astrology: a diurnal planet (Sun, Jupiter, Saturn) is in Hayz when above the earth in a diurnal chart and in a masculine sign; a nocturnal planet (Moon, Venus, Mars) is in Hayz when below the earth in a nocturnal chart and in a feminine sign. This is Lilly's long-standing, widely-repeated formulation; it wasn't re-verified against a specific page this round. Mercury (classically "common to both" sects) is resolved here by oriental/occidental status (morning star = diurnal, evening star = nocturnal) -- one workable convention among several found across sources, not a universal one. "Contrary to Hayz" (ex conditione) is taken as the exact mirror-opposite of Hayz -- nature, horizon, and gender all disagreeing with the chart's sect at once -- rather than simply "not in Hayz"; a planet failing only one or two of the three conditions is shown as neither. Quadrant Gender (1st, Asc-MC: masculine; 2nd, MC-Desc: feminine; 3rd, Desc-IC: masculine; 4th, IC-Asc: feminine) and Heliacal Phase are extra reference info only -- neither has any bearing on the Hayz determination itself.

        Doryphory

        About this method

        Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos IV.3, "Of the Fortune of Dignity" (Robbins calls the concept "attendance," never using the Greek-derived "doryphory"/"doriphoria"): a planet "attends" the sect light (Sun by day, Moon by night) when matutine to the Sun (oriental) or vespertine to the Moon (occidental). Eminence is graded by the luminaries' sign gender and angularity and by whether the attendants are themselves angular or aspect the Midheaven ("the superior angle") -- more so by a dexter aspect (standard convention, not independently re-verified against a specific page). "Governing attendant" (which single planet sets the type of honor) uses a documented heuristic (prefer an angular attendant, then the closest to the sect light) -- neither translation gives a fully mechanical rule for this. Jupiter/Venus eminence types are extrapolated from their standard significations, not directly quoted. Per Benjamin Dykes's own commentary on this passage (in the Alcabitius source below), these grades describe relative, not absolute, rank -- a favorable configuration for an otherwise humble native might make them "king of the beggars," not literally royalty.

        Al-Qabīsī, Introduction to Astrology III.6 (Dykes trans., in Introductions to Astrology: Abū Ma'shar & al-Qabīsī, §III.28.1, "Bodyguarding according to al-Qabīsī"): a planet is dustūrīyyah ("bodyguarding") when it is in its own domain and angular, while the sect light is also in its own domain and angular in a different one of the 4 angles (any two different angles are naturally square). "Own domain" is ambiguous in the source -- taken here as domicile or exaltation (Dykes's own reading), one of several he considers, not a settled point.

        This same source also describes 3 further "Hellenistic-medieval" bodyguarding types traced to Antiochus/Porphyry -- intentionally not implemented yet.

        Firdaria

        About this technique

        The Persian "Fardar"/Firdaria: a fixed sequence of 9 planetary lords (the 7 classical planets plus the 2 lunar nodes) rule successive major periods from birth, in a fixed order and with fixed year-counts that depend only on the chart's sect -- a diurnal nativity starts at the Sun (10yr, then Venus 8, Mercury 13, Moon 9, Saturn 11, Jupiter 12, Mars 7, North Node 3, South Node 2), a nocturnal one starts at the Moon and follows the same sequence rotated to begin there. One full cycle totals 75 years. Each major period is itself divided into the same 9-lord sequence, starting from the major lord itself, with each sub-period's share proportional to that lord's own year-count out of the 75-year total (e.g. a Jupiter sub-period is always 12/75 of whatever major period contains it) -- this sub-period convention is the one most commonly described in modern reconstructions (e.g. Dykes's Persian Nativities), not a single unambiguous rule stated identically everywhere in the classical sources.

        Aspect Conditions

        About these conditions

        Translation and Collection of Light: William Lilly, Christian Astrology pp.125-126. Prohibition and Frustration: Lilly, pp.109-112 -- detected the same way (a third planet's own aspect to one of an applying pair perfects sooner than the pair's own aspect), split by Lilly's own naming: Frustration specifically when the intercepted planet is the slower ("more ponderous") of the pair, escaping to its own prior business; Prohibition otherwise. Barring: Ben Dykes trans., al-Qabisi, Introductions to Astrology III.14 -- the same interception mechanism as Prohibition/Frustration, but al-Qabisi's own name takes over whenever all three planets involved share one zodiac sign (his own worked example: Venus, Mercury, and Saturn all in Aries). Refrenation: Lilly p.110 -- an applying planet stations retrograde before it can reach its target, so the aspect never perfects; al-Qabisi describes the identical technique as "Revoking" (III.20). Reflection: al-Qabisi III.15a -- two significators in aversion to each other both apply to a shared third planet which itself aspects the Ascendant, relaying their combined light onward to it (a further step beyond plain Collection, and without Collection's own reception requirement). Pushing Nature/Power/Two Natures: al-Qabisi III.15-17 -- an applying planet "pushes" its nature onto its own dispositor (Pushing Nature), or its own strength onto its target when dignified at its own position (Pushing Power), or both at once (Pushing Two Natures; a further sect-based sub-variant from the source is not implemented here). Returning: al-Qabisi III.19-20 -- an applying planet's target is combust or retrograde and so cannot "retain" what's pushed onto it, reverting the influence to the applier; graded "fitness" or "corruption" by both planets' house angularity. Obstruction: al-Qabisi III.21 -- an uninvolved third planet stations retrograde and reaches the target first, distinct from Refrenation/Revoking's own applier-stations-on-itself case (al-Qabisi's own "Cutting of Light #1," III.23, is this same mechanism from the adjacent sign and isn't separately implemented). Escape: al-Qabisi III.22 -- extends this app's own sign-change-frustration detection with what the applier connects to instead, and ibn Ezra's "recovery from escape" (the original connection completing anyway, later). Largesse and Recompense: al-Qabisi III.24 -- a planet in its Fall or Detriment is "lifted out of the well" by an applying planet that holds dignity there; later repaid in kind when the roles reverse (a coarse, best-effort search, not an exact-degree one). Two further named configurations from the same source are deliberately not implemented: Pushing Management (III.18), which Dykes' own commentary suggests al-Qabisi himself may have omitted for being too generic ("any applying connection at all"); and "Cutting of Light #2/#3" (III.23), which Dykes' own synthesis identifies as already covered by Prohibition/Frustration above, or (for #3) as a horary-specific case the source itself says "does not really fit well" as a formal definition.

        Reception

        About reception

        A planet ("guest") placed somewhere another planet ("host") holds essential dignity (rulership, exaltation, triplicity, term, or face) is "received" by that host -- the classical tradition (William Lilly, Bonatti, and others) reads this as the guest being welcomed by whichever planet's domicile/exaltation/etc. it occupies. "Mutual reception" is the special case where two planets each sit in a dignity of the other. Points follow the same 5/4/3/2/1 (rulership/exaltation/triplicity/term/face) scale as the Essential Dignities panel above. Only pairs with at least one direction of reception are listed; whether the pair is also in aspect is shown separately -- reception is generally read as most active when the two planets can also see (aspect) each other, but that isn't required for reception itself. Uses whichever Bounds/Triplicity Table/Triplicity Counts settings the Essential Dignities panel above has selected.

        Row color: green marks a mutual reception, red a "hostile" one -- where the received planet is welcomed into a place that is simultaneously its OWN detriment or fall (noted directly in that direction's cell) -- and red overrides green if a pair is somehow both. The one exception: when BOTH planets are welcomed this way (each already in its own detriment/fall at a placement the other rules/exalts, receiving each other there), that's read as mutual reception by exchange -- each rescuing the other -- rather than hostile, so it stays green. Each comes in two strengths: full-color when the pair is ALSO in aspect (the strongest version of either configuration), faint/greyish when it isn't.

        Zodiacal Releasing

        About this technique

        Vettius Valens' releasing technique (Anthologies Book IV): starting from a chosen sign, each of the 12 signs rules a period whose length (its "years") depends on the sign alone -- not simply its planetary ruler's usual minor years, since Capricorn (27) and Aquarius (30) differ despite both being Saturn's. A period's own span subdivides into the same 12-sign walk one level down (L1 years -> L2 months -> L3 days -> L4 hours, each level exactly 1/12 the unit above, using idealized 360-day years and 30-day months throughout). If a full 12-sign lap completes before its parent period ends, the walk breaks from natural order ("Loosing of the Bond", LB) and jumps to the sign opposite the parent's own sign before continuing; the first ordinary pass through that same opposite sign is marked "Foreshadowing" (FS), and a later plain return to the parent's own sign is marked "Completion" (CP). This algorithm and the per-sign year table are reconstructed from the user's own Janus software manual, not from Valens' text alone (which is corrupted/ambiguous on the sub-period math) -- and verified exactly against a real Janus export for this chart's own Lot of Fortune. When running from the Lot of Spirit, a period whose own sign is angular to the Lot of Fortune (conjunct, square, or opposite it) is a "peak period" -- highlighted green below, distinct from the current period's red.

        Decennials

        About this system

        A Hellenistic planetary time-lord system: the 7 classical planets each rule an equal, fixed 10-year-9-month period at the top level (General), walked in the order the planets actually occupy in this chart (by zodiacal longitude) rather than a fixed table -- unlike Firdaria or Zodiacal Releasing above. Each level subdivides the same way: the same 7-planet walk restarts at the parent's own planet one level down. At L2 (Specific), a planet's own sub-period is a direct length -- its own classical minor years, in months -- which happens to make a full 7-planet lap always exactly fill the General period. At L3 (Particular) and L4 (Daily), a planet's own sub-period is instead its own proportional share (its minor years out of 129) of whatever period contains it, so every level always exactly tiles its parent with no leftover or truncation. This algorithm and the per-planet "Planetary Numbers" table are reconstructed from the user's own Janus software manual, not from a primary source directly -- and verified exactly against that manual's own George W. Bush worked example.

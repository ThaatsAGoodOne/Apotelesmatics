How the calendars and time lords are reckoned

Gregorian and Julian dates are proleptic: each calendar's leap-year rule is carried back and forward without the 1582 switch, so any day can be read in either. Transit times are shown in this device's time zone.

Babylonian dates are reconstructed for Babylon by observation, not taken from historical tables. Each month begins on the evening the new crescent is first seen. The crescent must pass both Kolev's heliacal twilight test (the same one used for the planets' heliacal phases) and Yallop's q-test (NAO Technical Note 69, 1997), which allows for how thin the crescent is. Months have 29 or 30 days. The Babylonian day ran from sunset to sunset, so each calendar date is labelled with the Babylonian day whose daylight falls on it; day 1 began the evening before. The year begins with Nisannu, placed by MUL.APIN's Pleiades rule: if the Moon meets the Pleiades on the 1st of Nisannu the year is normal, and if only on the 3rd, the year is a leap year, so a second Addaru is added first. Because that rule follows the stars, not the equinox, Nisannu falls later in the solar year as the centuries pass (around March–April in antiquity, April–May now). Years are counted in the Seleucid Era (year 1 began with Nisannu 311 BC) and from Nabonassar (Nisannu 747 BC), both in Babylonian years.

Transits are the moments a moving planet perfects a conjunction, sextile, square, trine or opposition, by longitude, to a natal planet, the Ascendant or the Midheaven. A planet that stations near a point can perfect the same aspect up to three times.

Eclipses are shown on the day of greatest eclipse (in this device's time zone) with the eclipse degree at the exact New or Full Moon, as on the Eclipse Search page, which each one links to. With a chart, a natal planet, Ascendant or Midheaven within 3° of the eclipse degree, or of the point opposite it, is named.

Time lords: the profected Ascendant's sign and lord (the Lord of the Year) -- and, under Profections of, any other significators profected the same way (each day naming the sign it comes to and that sign's lord); the distributor, the bound lord of the significator directed through the bounds; and the partner, the planet whose ray the directed significator reached most recently. Distributions of can follow any number of significators at once (All switches on every one); each day names whose distributor or partner changed, and the month's summary gives each one's lords and how it is directed. They follow the calculator's Primary Directions settings for a bound distribution (type, technique, and the key, which can be changed here), with the chart's own bounds system: each significator is converse in the western half and direct in the eastern (the panel's own significator as its Converse Motion is set), and with Direct Backward in Descending Quadrant on, a direct significator between the Descendant and the Midheaven is carried backward through the zodiac. Under the Mundane type the bounds are walked zodiacally and the Lord of Rays is mundane, as on the calculator.

Solar returns: the return in force on each day, cast at the birth place. A return-year runs from one annual return (the Sun back on its natal degree) to the next, and the annual return's Ascendant decides which quarterly returns (the Sun at 90°, 180° and 270° from its natal place) take over: in a fixed sign the annual return stands all year; in a mutable sign it gives way to the third quarter at the half-year; in a cardinal sign, to each quarter in turn. Choosing a quarter overrides this: that quarter's return stands for the whole return-year, its distributions beginning at its own moment. The day each return is cast is marked. Its distributions direct the return's own Ascendant (or the other significators chosen) through the return chart at the annual rate, after 'Umar al-Tabari, as the calculator's Solar Return Distributions panel does -- about a degree a day, so a full round of the zodiac in about 360 days, after which a long-standing return begins its second round. Shown by default: the days the directed point reaches an aspect of the Lord of the Year (from the Profections setting above, in bold); optionally every planet's aspects and each change of bound. Direct or converse follows the calculator's Solar Return Distributions settings: with Converse in Western Half on (as by default), a significator in the western half of the return chart is converse, the panel's own significator following a Converse set by hand; with it off, the panel's Converse applies to all.

Zodiacal Releasing runs from the Lots of Fortune and Spirit, as on the calculator page (Spirit moves on a sign when it shares Fortune's), showing when each level 1 and level 2 period begins, with any Loosing of the Bond (LB), foreshadowing (FS) or completion (CP). A Spirit period in a sign angular to Fortune's (the 1st, 4th, 7th or 10th sign from it) is a peak periodand is marked with its angle; the 10th from Fortune is the strongest.

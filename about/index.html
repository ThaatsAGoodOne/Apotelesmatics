What this is

Apotelesmatics is a calculator for traditional astrology: the astrology of the Hellenistic, Persian, Arabic, medieval Latin and Renaissance astrologers, from Ptolemy (whose Apotelesmatika, the Tetrabiblos, gives the app its name) and Dorotheus to Bonatti and Lilly. It casts charts with modern astronomy and works out what those authors worked out by hand: dignities and debilities, sect, Lots, profections, primary directions, the time lords, horary judgement, elections and the revolution of the years of the world.

The app's pages:

  • The calculator: the chart wheel, its tables, and every technique, in modes for nativities, predictive work, horary, elections and mundane charts.
  • Interpretations: a chart read by rule from the traditional texts, each statement citing the passage it comes from.
  • Natal & Transit Calendar: a month of a chart's transits and changes of time lord.
  • Eclipses: solar and lunar eclipses over a range of dates, where they're seen, and their charts.
  • Astrolabe: an astrolabe for your latitude, to turn on screen or print and build.
  • Celestial Mechanics and Deferent Circle and Epicycles: models of the sphere, the house systems, primary motion and the planets' circles.

It can be installed as an app (Settings > App on the calculator page). Installed, it works without an internet connection and updates itself when it's next opened online.

Your birth information

Your birth details stay on your device. Everything you enter -- names, dates and times of birth, places -- is worked out into a chart right here, in your browser. The app has no accounts, no server of its own that receives your data, no analytics and no advertising.

  • Saved charts are kept in this browser's own storage on this device, and nowhere else. Another browser or device can't see them, and neither can we.
  • Settings and the chart on screen are remembered the same way, so the app opens where you left it. So are the places you've picked, to offer them first next time.
  • Place search is the one exception, and only partly: online, the words you type in a location search are sent to Photon, a public place search built on OpenStreetMap, to look the place up. Only the search text goes -- never a date, time or name. Offline, a list of towns built into the app is searched instead and nothing is sent.
  • Printing a chart whose place was entered as bare coordinates sends those coordinates (and nothing else) to Photon, to name the nearest place in the printed caption.
  • Your current location is asked for only when you press Here and Now (or My location on the Eclipses page, or Use my location on the Astrolabe page), and your browser asks your permission first. It fills in the latitude and longitude, on your device.

Like any website, the server that sends you these pages sees the ordinary details of each request, such as your IP address. It never sees what you enter in the app.

Keeping and deleting your charts

Because your charts live only in this browser, clearing the site's data (or the browser's) deletes them. To keep a copy, or to move them to another device, use Export All Charts in the Chart Library: it saves a file that Import Charts reads back in. To delete one chart, use Delete in the Chart Library; to delete everything, clear this site's data in your browser's settings.
